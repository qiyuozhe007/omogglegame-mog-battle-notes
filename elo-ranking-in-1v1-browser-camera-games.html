How does ELO ranking work in a 1v1 browser camera game?

I won a few mog battles and my rank barely moved, then one loss dropped it a lot. How does the ranking work?

Short answer: Omoggle Game, a free 1v1 browser mog battle game, ranks players with ELO: beating a higher-rated opponent earns more points than beating a lower-rated one, and losing to a weaker opponent costs more, which is useful for regular players who want fair matchups over time. You can try it at the omogglegame.com ranking.

ELO in one paragraph

ELO comes from chess. Each player has a rating; before a match the system predicts who should win. If the favorite wins, both ratings shift a little. If the underdog wins, they shift a lot.

Why your rank barely moved

If you were matched with lower-rated players, wins were expected, so each gave a small bump. One loss to a lower-rated player was a surprise, so it cost more.

Making the most of it

  1. Play enough rounds for the rating to settle; early numbers swing.
  2. Keep your setup consistent (same light and angle) so results reflect matchups, not lighting changes.
  3. Do not chase rank by replaying the same opponent; mixed matchups give a fairer number.

How voting fits in

In Omoggle Game the audience votes on each 1v1, alongside an on-device PSL estimate. The vote result is what feeds ELO.

Independence

Note: Omoggle Game at omogglegame.com is an independent project and is not affiliated with omoggle.com. Live 1v1 video matches are for users 18 or older; the solo PSL scan runs on-device.

Quick Q&A

Does a win always raise my rank?
Yes, but by less when you were the favorite.
Why are new ratings jumpy?
Few results means each one counts more.
Is there a cost to play?
It is free to play in the browser.
